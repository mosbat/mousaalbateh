1. Principle

My principle includes a diagram that helps determine whether a task should be left for humans, automated, AI automated or be hybrid (human-in-loop).

2. Problem

Organizations are adopting generative AI but used sometimes in scenarios that contradict with the desired business outcomes and lead to losses. 

3. Diagram
https://zenodo.org/records/24320246/files/mousa-principle-automation-v1_signed.pdf?download=1

4. Rationale

My diagram lays out a principle that will help in distinguishing between automation, AI and clarifies where humans should be kept in loop. Also, it clarifies when for certain business cases its better to keep certain tasks fully human.

5. Definitions

Human: The task should be handled fully by humans.

Automation: This means a code or program that follows traditional software development standards.

AI: This means the task is passed on to generative AI for processing.

Hybrid: This means that whether you choose AI or automation, a human must be involved for critical operations.

6. Example

According to [Castelo et al. (2023)](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/50/4/848/7100346?login=false), consumers evaluated the same service interaction less favorably when they were told they were interacting with a chatbot rather than a human employee. My diagram makes it clear that roles expecting human empathy should not be delegated to machines or non-humans.

7. Scope and limitations

My principle applies to common commercial use cases. It does not apply to defense related use cases. This is of course V1, so it might miss certain aspects as this is built based upon personal experiences as a Dev.

8. Related work

The ISO/IEC 42001 already addresses certain parts surrounding AI including ethics, human-in-loop. However my diagram is for practical use by decision makers and helps translate technical concepts into everyday business language to assist decision makers in making informed decisions.
