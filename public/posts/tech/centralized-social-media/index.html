Why We Need to Reduce Our Dependency on Centralized Social Media Platforms

I have been on social media platforms for quite a while. I have been observing so many issues surrounding social media platforms specially related to cybersecurity (e.g. bots) and achieving the balance between satisfying their regular users as well as staying compliant with laws and regulations.

Social Media Platforms Became Too Big
#

Social media platforms have grown both in scale and size to such a level that the everyday user tries to find everything in a single place. YouTube for example has around 2.77 billion users while TikTok has roughly 2.33 billion users. Of course, we didn’t mention yet other platforms such as LinkedIn, Instagram, etc….

It has become nearly a general knowledge that to get visibility its almost a must to have a presence on social media platforms by setting up an account and posting content on regular basis.

As social media platforms kept growing over the years, regular web traffic to websites of both individuals and small businesses dropped dramatically pushing small business owners and creators to either abandon hosting their own website or adopting strategies that increases the cost of visibility by relying on social media platforms to certain extent.

Challenges
#

The main challenge facing small businesses, creators and professionals is visibility. This challenge rose due to accumulation of several problems. We will dive into each problem one by one.

Visibility Became a Full Time Job
#

Most of the social media platforms capitalize on regular users uploading content such as entertainment, news, memes and even short movies created by indie studios.

The social media platform’s business model requires users to keep posting in order to keep the existing users and potentially acquire more users.

This business model works for big players or creators in the entertainment industry. However, for the vegetables seller, this isn’t easy to keep doing.

Companies with big capital can hire studios and marketing agencies who operate in that model but this isn’t helpful for small businesses or small creators who are just trying to take off.

Lack of Transparency and Shadow Banning
#

Unfortunately, most social media platforms are nto transparent on why certain content will be displayed to their user base and why others won’t. The algorithms are normally proprietary and people are left guessing what works and what doesn’t unless you pay.

100s if not 1000s of users get flagged either intentionally or by accident because of their content which leads to loss of visibility and potentially loss of revenue.

It is understandable that certain type of content would lead to bans or shadow bans. However, social media companies quite often fail to explain why a ban happened or give false reasons and are able to double down on their decisions since content you upload to their platforms becomes partially their property and they reserve the right to choose not to show that content even if it is not violating any laws.

Shadow banning is potentially one of the worst ever tools social media platforms use against their users because the user won’t be aware that they have been effectively banned till its too late. This leads to loss of time/revenue for a lot of small businesses or creators.

Humans Vs. Bots
#

Social media platforms are constantly fighting with bot accounts that became more sophisticated with generative AI (aka Narrow Artificial Intelligence). Bot operators can generate 1000s of fake accounts and simulate behavior of real humans for all kinds of purposes (mostly malicious).

This means, the creators cannot even figure out if their content is being seen by actual human users or bots.

Search Engines Are Also Part of the Problem
#

Most search engines are not immune from criticism. Search engines are operating in similar ways to centralized social media platforms.

If you create your own website, you are still under the mercy of search engines for visibility. The real conflict of interest takes place when the search engine is also offering Ads since this incentives the search engine company to make organic reach more difficult.

Moreover, if search engines won’t improve, people will go back to social media platforms.

Search engines also require a much deeper dive but for the sake of this article’s scope, I’ll keep my focus for now on social media platforms particularly.

Organic Vs. Paid Reach
#

Just like search engines, if the social media platform is offering Ads, they are less incentivized to make organic reach meaningfully useful. This is why, on many social media platforms, content seem to have an invisible cap on how many views the content gets unless users actively share it to try to force the algorithm to push it.

Aftermath
#

This whole situation gave centralized social media platforms huge leverage and power with users having little to no recourse because those platforms operate as private companies, not government and they could undermine certain rights via company policies and community guidelines that are vague and not specific enough. Social media platform users have no say at all in those policies.

Small businesses,indie creators and professionals are overshadowed by parties with big capital who are able to flood the algorithms with Ads or dedicated agencies who are able to keep their content running 24/7.

Adding insult to injury, small businesses and indie creators who do manage to pierce the glass ceiling, could lose all their followers for minor infringements and their followers won’t even realize that one of their favorite creators got banned.

This means, if a creator worked hard to build their audience and reached finally a meaningful milestone, all of this could be gone in an instance either by malicious actors mass reporting or a moderator who decided to ban the account. Even if the ban happened by mistake, companies will try to avoid admitting it to avoid potential liability as users could take a legal path if the ban led to losses.

Why This Matters to All of Us
#

If you have an account on any of the major social media platforms, you are actually an active participant in this system. Even if you are not a creator, this does impact you indirectly. The more time you spend on social media platform, the less time you spend discovering content that could have benefited you more.

This is one of those tricky situations where our complacency and silence neither incentives the platforms to change nor helps small businesses and creators who may have been treated unfairly by those platforms. One day this could be you, me or one of our loved ones.

Solutions
#

There are many solutions but this requires grass root movements and spreading of awareness through friends and family. This is not a one person fight but its the entire society.

Digital Sovereignty
#

Since each and every single person have a different needs, the question you may need to ask yourself is the following:

“If I got banned now or in future, what will be the impact and how to protect myself”

This question is so important because once you thinking and analyzing how this impacts you, you will realize that you have been exposed to the Sirens. You will realize that there is a lot of work to do.

You may begin by creating your own website. Today with AI tools, this should be a piece of cake. If you are not necessarily a person who enjoys frontend, you may consider some platforms to build your site.

Incentivize Viewers to Return to Your Site
#

Another tactic you could use, is offer users who revisit your website offers such as coupons, bonuses or free gifts.

Create QR Business Cards
#

Another way to get traffic is by creating QR business cards. When you meet people, use your website as your frontend and identity. The physical card makes it hard for them to forget you completely. However, I’d recommend using instead QR on your screen in order to reduce paper waste and for the environment.

Plan for Your Own Website
#

You may use social media platforms to get exposure or visibility but remember that your goal is actually to encourage users to be more curious and willing to explore outside their comfort zone.

Treat your social media account as something you could lose any minute for whatever reason.

Warning

Never create content and make it exclusive to the platform. Always create your content on your own devices and keep the originals in secure offline backup.

Tip

You can post content first on your own website and then try to post the same content on social media platforms but you do not need to make the social media platforms wealthier. You can provide them only with partial content and let users choose to visit your site to access the full deal.
